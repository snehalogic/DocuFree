Cloud computing is   the on - demand delivery of IT resources — such as servers, storage, databases,  and software — over the internet with pay - as - you - go pricing, rather than owning and maintaining  physical data centers . It enables companies to lower costs, scale infrastructure instantly, and access  services from providers like AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud.  Key Aspects of Cloud Computing  •   Service Models:  o   IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service):   Provides fundamental computing, network, and  storage resources.  o   PaaS (Platform as a Service):   Offers a development environment to build and deploy  apps.  o   SaaS (Software as a Service):   Delivers software applications over the internet (e.g.,  Salesforce, Office 365).  •   Deployment Models:  o   Public Cloud:   Services delivered over the internet to the public, like   AWS   or   Google  Cloud .  o   Private Cloud:   Dedicated infrastructure for a single organization.  o   Hybrid Cloud:   A combination of public and private clouds.  •   Core Benefits:  o   Cost Efficiency:   Shifts capital expenses (CapEx) to operating expenses (OpEx).  o   Speed & Agility:   Resources can be provisioned in minutes.  o   Global Scale:   Ability to expand to different regions instantly.  o   Reliability:   Data backup   and disaster recovery are simplified .  Common Examples  •   Storage:   Google Drive, Dropbox.  •   Streaming:   Netflix, Spotify.  •   Productivity:   Gmail, Slack.  •   Infrastructure:   AWS, Azure, Google Cloud.
