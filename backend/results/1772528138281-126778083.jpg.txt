Title of paper:
Effect of Explainable Artificial Intelligence on Trust of Mental Health
Professionals in an Al-Based System for Suicide Prevention
Introduction
Artificial Intelligence (Al) is increasingly being integrated into mental health care, particularly for early
detection of suicidal ideation (SI). Al systems, especially those based on Large Language Models (LLMs),
can analyze patient-generated text and identify warning signs remotely. However, despite their
technological potential, the success of such systems depends largely on whether Mental Health
Professionals (MHPs) trust and accept them.
One of the major concerns in medical Al is the “black-box” nature of many models, where decisions are
made without clear explanations. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) aims to address this issue by
providing transparency through techniques such as LIME, which highlight important words influencing
predictions. This study investigates how XAl affects trust among MHPs using an Al system called
Boamente, designed to detect suicidal ideation in Brazilian Portuguese texts.
Methodology
The study followed an online, experimental, randomized, single-masked research design. A total of 78
Brazilian mental health professionals (psychologists and psychiatrists) participated. Participants had
clinical experience and some familiarity with Al concepts.
They were randomly divided into four groups:

* Groups A and B: System without explanations

+ Groups C and D: System with explanations

«Some groups also received a 20-minute educational video explaining how the Al system works
Participants interacted with the Boamente web interface and evaluated the system's predictions
regarding suicidal ideation. Trust levels were measured using Likert-scale questionnaires. The quality of
explanations was assessed using the System Causability Scale (SCS). Statistical analyses such as ANCOVA
and General Linear Models (GLM) were used to evaluate the effects of explanations and educational
intervention on trust.
Results
The findings revealed several important outcomes:

1. Providing explanations significantly reduced trust.

Participants who received explanations (Groups C and D) reported lower trust compared to
those who did not receive explanations.
